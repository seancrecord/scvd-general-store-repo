That record remains closed; no new result is inferred from this repair.

A future plan can now add `buyer_setup_guidance: true` alongside `capability.setup_guidance: "standalone-node-v1"`. The buyer gets the same setup prefix used by qualification: available native tools, standalone Node filesystem methods, existing session directories, batching of already permitted operations and bounded source reads. The original buyer task, source review, voluntary decision, exact package permission, budget and evidence obligations follow unchanged. There is no supplied verifier program, source selection, signature answer, receipt decision or spending recommendation.

The new flag must be explicit true and requires the existing qualification condition. Omitting it preserves prior buyer prompts byte for byte; malformed or unsupported conditions fail validation. The qualification prompt itself is unchanged, and the full plan fingerprint prevents an earlier qualification from authorizing buyers under the new condition. Offline recipient prompts and permissions are unchanged. The guidance contains no service identity, including when used in an unbranded lane.

Three controls failed before implementation: invalid conditions were accepted, buyer setup guidance was absent, and an opted-in unbranded prompt lacked the guidance. The old-plan qualification binding already rejected reuse. After implementation, all 85 focused and 290 buyer controls pass, along with typecheck and both Worker bundle checks. Seven prompts from the closed September 30 archive (four buyers, two online qualifications and the offline protocol) reproduce byte for byte without the new flag. ## Signed originals remain a separate question

The earlier Claude r1 report had a different failure: it treated missing merchant-signed offers as absence of verifiable evidence and did not follow the SCVD-signed snapshot links. The complete retained host-history result included `evidence_scope` and its instructions, and the guide already says an unsigned merchant offer does not rule out a signed SCVD observation. This pass does not claim missing documentation, fix the merchant, add another warning, or give the experiment's buyer a desired verdict. A tool-capability pass is not evidence-acquisition or interpretation acceptance.

The remaining investigation should follow whether a buyer selects and retains the linked signed original, distinguishes its signer from the merchant, and explains the scope after verification. Retain the actual responses and errors; a controller's later verification cannot fill missing native acquisition or recipient understanding.
