## §7 Facilitator Interface — what it says

- **7.1 `POST /verify`** is read-only: it "validates payment state but
  MUST NOT commit payment state or write onchain state". Resource
  servers invoke it only when the resolved flow's ordering includes it;
  `upfront` and `escrow` omit it.
- **7.2 `POST /settle`** durably commits payment state — and
  **commitment need not be an onchain write**. For client-prepaid
  methods, settle MAY bind a payment proof to the request after
  *read-only observation* of ledger or backend state. A settle need not
  be the final charge: it may establish an escrow or record a charge.
- `/settle` **MAY be invoked more than once** for one payment. A scheme
  defining multiple settles MUST specify how the facilitator tells them
  apart; that distinction is usually server-led.
- **7.2.1** adds an `EXTENSION-RESPONSES` sidechannel header, base64
  JSON, explicitly **not** part of the response body and **not**
  forwarded to buyers.
- **7.3 `GET /supported`** requires `kinds`, `extensions` and `signers`,
  the last a map of CAIP-2 patterns to public signer addresses.

## Against what we publish: clean

Our `settlement-response-v1` reader cites §5.3.2 and §9. Both citations
hold at this tip:

- §5.3.2 requires `transaction` as a **string**, "empty string if no
  transaction was broadcast", and **MUST be non-empty when
  `errorReason` is `settlement_pending`". Our `transaction-string` and
  `pending-names-transaction` checks say exactly that, including the
  empty-when-nothing-broadcast wording.
- `errorReason` is omitted on success. Our `error-reason-on-success`
  check reads a response carrying both as self-contradictory and
  returns `unresolved` rather than `failed`, which is the conservative
  direction.
- A `success: true` carrying an empty `transaction` reads **settled**,
  not defective — "that is its word; the chain's copy is what an
  attestation reads." Under §7.2 that response is not merely tolerable
  but explicitly legal for client-prepaid methods, so a reader scoring
  it as a defect would be wrong. Ours does not.

No claim of ours needed changing.

## What §7 adds to this morning's work

`SETTLEMENT_RESIDUAL` in `scripts/lib/paid-doors.mjs` was written from
observation: a door advertising a scheme other than `exact` can be paid
with no direct USDC transfer to its advertised `payTo` ever appearing,
because a batch or escrow scheme moves buyer funds into a contract.

§7.2 says the same thing from the other end, as protocol: settlement
"need not be an onchain write", and may bind a proof after read-only
observation. So that residual is not a quirk we happened to trip over
in one door — it is sanctioned behaviour, and any chain-side instrument
that reads `ZERO_OBSERVED` as "not paid" is contradicting the
specification, not just missing a case.

The residual's wording stands. Its **status** is upgraded: from a
caveat we found by running something, to a caveat the spec requires.
