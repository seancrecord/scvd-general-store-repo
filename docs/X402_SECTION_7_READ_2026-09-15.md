## §7 Facilitator Interface — what it says

- **7.1 `POST /verify`** is read-only: it "validates payment state but
  MUST NOT commit payment state or write onchain state". Resource
  servers invoke it only when the resolved flow's ordering includes it;
  `upfront` and `escrow` omit it.
- **7.2 `POST /settle`** durably commits payment state — and
  **commitment need not be an onchain write**. For client-prepaid
  methods, settle MAY bind a payment proof to the request after
  *read-only observation* of ledger or backend state. A settle need not
  be the final charge: it may establish an escrow or record a charge.
- `/settle` **MAY be invoked more than once** for one payment. A scheme
  defining multiple settles MUST specify how the facilitator tells them
  apart; that distinction is usually server-led.
- **7.2.1** adds an `EXTENSION-RESPONSES` sidechannel header, base64
  JSON, explicitly **not** part of the response body and **not**
  forwarded to buyers.
- **7.3 `GET /supported`** requires `kinds`, `extensions` and `signers`,
  the last a map of CAIP-2 patterns to public signer addresses.

## Against what we publish: clean

Our `settlement-response-v1` reader cites §5.3.2 and §9. Both citations
hold at this tip:

- §5.3.2 requires `transaction` as a **string**, "empty string if no
  transaction was broadcast", and **MUST be non-empty when
  `errorReason` is `settlement_pending`". Our `transaction-string` and
  `pending-names-transaction` checks say exactly that, including the
  empty-when-nothing-broadcast wording.
- `errorReason` is omitted on success. Our `error-reason-on-success`
  check reads a response carrying both as self-contradictory and
  returns `unresolved` rather than `failed`, which is the conservative
  direction.
- A `success: true` carrying an empty `transaction` reads **settled**,
  not defective — "that is its word; the chain's copy is what an
  attestation reads." Under §7.2 that response is not merely tolerable
  but explicitly legal for client-prepaid methods, so a reader scoring
  it as a defect would be wrong. Ours does not.

No claim of ours needed changing.

## What §7 adds to this morning's work

`SETTLEMENT_RESIDUAL` in `scripts/lib/paid-doors.mjs` was written from
observation: a door advertising a scheme other than `exact` can be paid
with no direct USDC transfer to its advertised `payTo` ever appearing,
because a batch or escrow scheme moves buyer funds into a contract.

§7.2 says the same thing from the other end, as protocol: settlement
"need not be an onchain write", and may bind a proof after read-only
observation. So that residual is not a quirk we happened to trip over
in one door — it is sanctioned behaviour, and any chain-side instrument
that reads `ZERO_OBSERVED` as "not paid" is contradicting the
specification, not just missing a case.

The residual's wording stands. Its **status** is upgraded: from a
caveat we found by running something, to a caveat the spec requires.

## `authority` §7 — The preimage binding (enforced, not asserted)

A `mandateDigest` on a payment object is a *claim*. The binding makes it
a fact, using the one payer-chosen 32-byte slot each scheme's signature
already covers:

```
B = SHA-256( "x402-mandate-binding/1\n" || UTF8( mandateDigest || "\n" || paymentId ) )
```

carried in `nonce` for EIP-3009 (`0x` + lowerhex), in `nonce` for
Permit2 (big-endian uint256, decimal), and in `InvoiceID` for XRPL
(uppercase hex).

Three rules in it that are worth more than the formula:

**1. The prefix is load-bearing.** `mandateDigest` MUST enter the
preimage as the complete §5 string, keeping its literal lowercase
`"sha256:"` prefix — exactly 71 characters. Implementations MUST NOT
strip it to bare hex. That is the entire subject of #3220, and it is
the classic canonicalization break: two implementations that disagree
about whether a prefix is part of the input produce different digests
from the same mandate and neither can tell which one is wrong.

**2. Verify from the settled artifact, never from a presented field.**
Counterparties MUST check the slot against the signed authorization or
the transaction itself. This is our own house rule arriving as
protocol, and it is the same rule our launch check follows when it
reads settlement from the chain rather than from the `PAYMENT-RESPONSE`
header a seller hands it.

**3. At-most-once is rail-dependent, and XRPL is the exception.**
EIP-3009 and Permit2 consume the nonce, so a `(mandate, paymentId)`
settles at most once. **XRPL does not enforce `InvoiceID` uniqueness**:
two `Payment` transactions carrying the same InvoiceID can both settle.
So any consumer deriving cumulative spend from XRPL evidence MUST
de-duplicate observed settlements by `(mandateDigest, paymentId)`, and
MUST treat two distinct validated Payments bearing one derived
InvoiceID as spend *exceeding* the presented log.

## Against what we publish

**We do not implement the `authority` extension.** No `mandateDigest`,
no binding preimage, nowhere in `src/` or `defects/`. So the #3220
prefix-stripping bug is not a bug we can have, and nothing here needed
changing.

Our own `mandates` service solves the neighbouring problem — a signed,
third-party-held record of what was authorized before an agent acts —
and is deliberately *not* this extension. It registers itself as
chain-of-custody rather than truth-of-intent. If we ever claim the
extension, §7 is the conformance surface we would be measured on, and
the vectors in `authority-vectors.json` are the fixtures to measure
against.

**Rule 3 is the one to pass on.** It is a counting rule: an XRPL spend
figure that does not de-duplicate by `(mandateDigest, paymentId)` is
not merely imprecise, it is an overcount the rail will not prevent.
Anyone reading XRPL ledgers for settlement evidence — including the
operator whose pinned boundaries in the current exchange are XRPL
ledger indices — is reading a surface where the spec says the rail
does not enforce what a reader might assume it does.
