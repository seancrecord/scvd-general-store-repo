## Recovery repairs

**B-RSTATUS / LA-01.** A free authenticated status read distinguishes `recovery_state: pending`, `ready`, `not_settled` and `resolved`, independently of payment and human-work completion. Pending reads carry `next_action: read_purchase_status`, a suggested `retry_after_seconds`, and instructions for HTTP or MCP. The interval is explicitly advisory, not a completion promise. Ready reads return the original fulfillment; an accepted human order is still an order. A recorded resolution stops the recovery-poll instructions. The existing delivery-evidence field remains intact: payment alone cannot establish delivery. Storage, alarms and settlement behavior are unchanged.

**B-RDOOR / LA-02.** The authenticated recovery handle names `original_door` and `original_path`. A mismatched replay explains the interface boundary, marks the refusal non-temporary, says repeating that request will not recover the purchase, and directs the buyer to the free private status read or original request. HTTP returns 409 instead of 503 through both accepted-verifier and expired/refused-verifier recovery paths. MCP retains its protocol error result. Scope, signature and ownership guards remain intact; this change does not authorize cross-interface fulfillment. The same guidance and output properties are shared across OpenAPI, MCP, skill and llms surfaces. The public error vocabulary includes the refusal.

The new tests use signed local fixtures across all five enabled test rails and HTTP plus both MCP profiles. They exercise immediate settled-but-not-ready status, original artifact retrieval after recovery, unknown and unpaid records, wrong credentials, both directions of cross-interface replay, accepted and expired verifier paths, same-interface recovery, no second settlement, and published recovery instructions. Existing refund-resolution controls now assert terminal recovery guidance too.

## Reconciliation defects — original reproduction, now repaired locally

**B-RCAP — unrelated Approval becomes the observed ceiling.** A receipt with an unambiguous canonical-USDC AuthorizationUsed/Transfer pair for 3 USDC plus a same-owner Approval of 10 USDC to another spender produces `within_cap`, `cap_observed: true`, `chain_same_tx_approval`, and a 10 USDC ceiling. The result is the same whether that approval precedes or follows the paired transfer. A transfer followed by an otherwise unbound approval also receives that observed ceiling. The resulting observation signature verifies. Current owner filtering and transfer ambiguity flags do not bind the approval to the selected movement; approval precedence also overrides the already-established fixed authorization.

Repair now implemented: preserve independently paired EIP-3009 evidence and refuse to promote an approval into a transfer's observed ceiling without evidence of the spending relationship. A receipt's Transfer event does not identify the spender or allowance used; ordering or owner equality alone cannot establish that relationship. The signed claim, public description and correction were changed together. Existing historical bytes remain unchanged.

**B-RCONTEXT — reconciliation signs unestablished receipt context.** Direct-service probes return signed reconciliations when the provider supplies another transaction hash, omits the receipt hash, reports a receipt block above the observed head, or supplies an invalid receipt status. The invalid-status case becomes `no_settlement`; the others become `no_discretion` while naming the requested hash. The wrong-chain probe also signs without ever asking `eth_chainId`: its configured wrong-chain answer is not consumed. This demonstrates absent chain establishment, not a recorded wrong-chain incident in production.

Repair now implemented: apply the existing shared receipt/chain-context checks to this reader and prove that both purchase interfaces refuse before settlement, with usable retry advice. Preserve explicit-null not-found semantics. The malformed-head probe already fails to return an artifact through the JCS finite-number guard; the review does not claim that every malformed response is currently signed. Provider assertions are still not consensus proofs.
