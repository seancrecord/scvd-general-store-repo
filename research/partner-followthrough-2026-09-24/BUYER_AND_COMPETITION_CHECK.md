## Browserbase: real reach evidence, unproven need for us

Browserbase’s July 2 story attributes more than 300,000 sessions since April
to Airtable’s Hyperagent. It also describes session replay, structured logs
and live view. These are vendor-reported platform-use claims; they do not
show that Airtable uses Browserbase’s x402 gateway or lacks internal QA.
[Airtable case study](https://dev.browserbase.com/blog/case-study-hyperagent).

The customer index names Ramp, Vercel and Commure among other examples.
This supports investigating enterprise distribution through Browserbase,
not claiming those firms as SCVD prospects with a known payment-path gap.
[Customer stories](https://www.browserbase.com/customer-stories).

Inference to test: a repeatable outside buyer record could help with defects
before session creation or discrepancies across documentation, discovery,
payment and usable output. The company’s existing observability makes a
generic logs/dashboard pitch weak. Ask its reviewer for one decision their
current tools do not settle. If they cannot name one, hold the study.

## Merit: distribution potential, not a proven enterprise buyer

Merit’s developer page describes registries and an agent wallet/MCP product.
That is evidence of surfaces where a correction might reach other builders.
The page is not customer-volume, spend or willingness-to-pay evidence.
[Developer resources](https://www.merit.systems/developers).

The verified schema/resolver control is the current contribution. Do not
infer that the deployed registration service uses the exact tested package.
Do not build a second registry to serve a hypothesis that a small fixture
and retest can resolve first.

## The next decision

Keep Merit’s sent note as the first contribution, qualify BiX for a small
practical exchange, and retain Browserbase as the enterprise-reach candidate.
Track reach, demonstrated problem, partner participation and repeated use in
separate fields. One company’s large customer base cannot fill the other three.
