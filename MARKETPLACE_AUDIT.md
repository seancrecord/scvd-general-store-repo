# MARKETPLACE_AUDIT.md — the verbiage and rules audit for the evolution

The keeper's directive, 2026-08-07: "We need to be the trusted,
verified general goods marketplace for the agent community. Audit all
verbiage, rule files, house rules — don't worry as much about the
products listed as what the change enables."

This file is that audit. It operates under HOUSE_RULES' own law —
rules change only by keeper decision, logged with a date — so nothing
here amends anything. It names every place the standing canon
ENABLES the marketplace, every place it COLLIDES with it, and every
GAP where the marketplace era needs a rule that does not exist yet.
Each collision ends in a ⚑ decision that is the keeper's alone.

The one-line finding, up front: **the pivot is not a reversal of the
canon — it is rule 23a applied at scale.** "The store's one real
product is INDEPENDENT SIGNED OBSERVATION; every serious item is that
primitive pointed at a different moment." A verified marketplace is
that primitive pointed at other people's goods. Most of the canon
survives contact. Five pieces do not, and they are all keeper-settled
decisions that must be consciously amended, not drifted past.

=====================================================================
PART 2 — THE COLLISIONS. Five settled decisions the pivot reverses.
=====================================================================

## Collision 1 — Rule 23a: "WE NEVER HOLD, JUDGE, OR PROMISE-TO-ACT-LATER"

The Sorting Line is the store's constitutional clause, and three of
the memo's tiers walk into it:

- **Verified reselling** (Tier 2A/2B): the buyer pays us, we pay the
  upstream. That is money HELD between two parties, however briefly
  — not escrow, but escrow's little cousin, and 23a's objection
  (infrastructure and liability) applies at reduced scale. Worse:
  "we'll flag it the day it degrades" is a PROMISE-THAT-MUST-FIRE-
  IN-THE-FUTURE — the exact class 23b declined as dead-man's
  switches and SLA monitors.
- **Verified Skills** (Tier 2C): "we review skills for safety" is
  JUDGMENT, sold. A badge that says "safe" is a ruling between the
  skill's author and its buyer, and 23a names the first wrong
  ruling as a lawsuit surface and a reputation-killer.
- **Continuous conformance monitoring** (the CORTX-hybrid): the
  declined SLA-monitor class, verbatim.

**But the canon already bent here once, and the precedent is the
answer.** `standing_watch` shipped 2026-W32: a week of signed hourly
checks — a bounded, PREPAID promise-to-act-later, with the missed
passes published against ourselves. Nobody repealed 23a to ship it;
it threaded the rule by being bounded (7 days, then done), prepaid
(no ongoing billing relationship), and honest about gaps (a watchman
who leaves his naps out of the log isn't one). That is the template
that lets most of the memo through:

- Not "we'll flag it when it degrades" (open-ended) but "a signed
  conformance watch on service X, N days, $Y, gaps counted against
  us" (bounded, renewable by the buyer's choice, never by ours).
- Not "verified safe" (a standing judgment) but "conformance-checked
  on DATE against THESE criteria, report signed, criteria public"
  (a dated observation — exactly what /attestation already does for
  our own artifacts).

⚑ KEEPER DECISION 1: amend 23a with a dated carve-out naming the
standing_watch shape — "bounded, prepaid, gap-published watches are
observation, not promises" — or rule that the carve-out was a
one-off. The marketplace's verification tier lives or dies on this
sentence. Recommended: amend, codifying what already shipped.

⚑ KEEPER DECISION 2: reselling means being IN the money flow between
buyer and upstream. Options: (a) true reselling (we hold margin and
liability), (b) verified referral (buyer pays upstream directly; we
sell the signed conformance report and the watch, never touching the
flow), (c) decline. Option (b) keeps 23a fully intact, keeps rule 41
exposure flat, and monetizes the same verification work — the margin
is smaller but it is OUR product (observation) rather than somebody
else's product with our sticker. Recommended: (b) first, (a) only
with named legal review under rule 41.

## Collision 2 — /becoming SETTLED: "Will this become infrastructure? No. It stays a shop."

The keeper's own settled answer, published, with /attestation
repeating "THIS IS A SHOP, NOT INFRASTRUCTURE and will keep saying
it" (attestation-spec.ts:244). The stated reason is architectural:
other people's uptime depending on one keeper hands strangers a
pager pointed at a man with a day job.

A verified reseller of browser automation IS infrastructure by this
page's own definition — an agent mid-task depends on our till being
up to reach Browser Use. A marketplace of verified goods is
infrastructure by construction.

This is the deepest collision in the audit because it is not a rule,
it is a published promise to evaluators, load-bearing on /becoming
and /attestation both. It CANNOT be quietly deleted — an evaluator
who watched us say "will keep saying it" and then stop saying it has
caught us drifting. The honest move is a dated, signed reversal on
/corrections-adjacent terms: "we said X on 2026-07-30; the evidence
that changed our mind is Y; here is what protects the buyer now that
X no longer holds" — and the architectural objection must actually
be ANSWERED, not waved at (succession key, published degradation
behavior, the wind-down page extended to cover in-flight marketplace
obligations).

⚑ KEEPER DECISION 3: reverse the SETTLED answer with a signed, dated
reasoning trail, or scope the marketplace to stay under it (the
verified-referral model of Decision 2(b) arguably does: reports and
watches are goods you leave with; nobody's uptime depends on us).

## Collision 3 — Rule 21: the barbell doctrine

"Stock the penny shelf and the human-labor shelf; distrust the
middle." The memo's Tier 2 ($0.10–$5.00) IS the middle, named and
priced. The barbell was derived from demand research in July; the
memo's evidence (average x402 transfer $0.23, Browser Use at
$0.02/hr real volume) is newer and points the other way.

This one is clean: rule 20 says the ledger outranks all research —
OURS INCLUDED. The barbell was a research posture, and the market
has since produced a ledger.

⚑ KEEPER DECISION 4: retire rule 21 with a date, or restate it as
"distrust the middle WITHOUT a demand tag" — which collapses it into
rule 19 and lets the evidence rule govern everything. Recommended:
the restatement; it preserves the discipline while unblocking the
tier.

## Collision 4 — Rule 19: the evidence rule

"No new item without a demand tag." The memo proposes stocking 3–5
resold APIs "this week" from a catalog read — which is desk
reasoning, the precise thing rule 19 gates. The rule is GOOD and the
memo is premature by its standard: the Browser Use datum is a demand
tag for browser automation; a Circle catalog listing is not a demand
tag for email verification.

No amendment needed — but the pivot needs a lighter instrument the
rule doesn't define: a way to VALIDATE marketplace demand cheaply
before stocking (a "coming to the shelf?" page per candidate, a
request counter, the mailbox). The store already has /api/request.

⚑ KEEPER DECISION 5: none required. Flagged so nobody treats rule 19
as suspended by the pivot. Each Tier 2 item still earns its tag —
Browser Use has one; the rest of the Circle catalog does not yet.

## Collision 5 — "No reputation score, ours or anybody's"

NOT_CLAIMED (trust-signals.ts) and /what both state it, and rule 22's
spirit backs it. A Verified Skills badge program IS a reputation
system — for skills. The line as written becomes false the day the
first badge ships.

The distinction that survives: the store scores ARTIFACTS AND
CONDUCT AT A POINT IN TIME (a conformance report is dated
observation), never ACCUMULATING SCORES ON ACTORS. "This skill
passed these checks on this date, signed" is /attestation's
vocabulary. "This author is 4.7 stars" is the thing the canon
rejects and should keep rejecting — it is unverifiable, gameable,
and reaches into relationships that aren't ours (the /becoming
line the store will not cross).

⚑ KEEPER DECISION 6: amend the NOT_CLAIMED line to state that
distinction ("no accumulating reputation scores on any actor; dated,
signed, criteria-published checks on artifacts are what we sell
instead") — BEFORE the first badge ships, not after.

=====================================================================
PART 3 — THE GAPS. Rules the marketplace era needs that don't exist.
=====================================================================

1. **A definition of "verified," per badge class, in /attestation
   style.** The store's scrupulousness about that word is already on
   record (the AIR entry: "VERIFIED BY USER: self-attested, NOT
   verified by the organization, and the record says so"). The same
   scrupulousness must apply to every badge WE issue: exactly what
   was checked, when, against what criteria, what it does NOT prove,
   and what retires it. A "verified" claim without its per-class
   attestation page is the auto-refund incident waiting to happen at
   marketplace scale.

2. **An upstream dependency policy.** /stack lists what we depend on
   with failure modes. Every verified/resold listing adds an
   upstream whose failure becomes OUR event. Rule needed: no
   marketplace listing without its /stack entry, its failure mode,
   and its effect on the buyer stated on the listing itself.

3. **A markup honesty rule.** If the store ever resells (Decision
   2(a)), the margin is disclosed — "upstream charges $0.10, we
   charge $0.15, the nickel is the watch" — because a store whose
   whole moat is radical honesty cannot have exactly one number it
   is coy about. If verified referral (2(b)), no rule needed; the
   report price IS the price.

4. **Badge revocation and the corrections pipeline.** A verified
   thing that goes bad needs a published path: dated de-badging, the
   reason on record, buyers who relied on the badge notified how
   (the guestbook? the letter? nothing?). Un-verifying quietly is
   worse than never verifying.

5. **A conflict rule.** The store cannot verify a thing it profits
   from reselling without naming the conflict. If both tiers ever
   coexist, the listing says which relationship applies. (Cleanest:
   never both on the same item.)

6. **Rule 23a's money-flow boundary, written down.** Whatever
   Decision 2 lands on, the boundary between "payment passed
   through" and "funds held" needs one paragraph with the same
   precision as the escrow refusal — because a stranger reading the
   marketplace WILL ask, and today the answer lives nowhere.

should change before the facts change (words follow facts — the

is where they are watched:

  human labour" — the word "small" is doing barbell-era work.
- `what.ts` Q1: "A small general store" — same.
- `becoming.ts` SETTLED[0] and `attestation-spec.ts:244` — the
  infrastructure denial (Collision 2). The single highest-priority
  string in this audit: it is a PROMISE about future speech.
- NOT_CLAIMED: "No reputation score, ours or anybody's" (Collision
  5); "No third-party security audit of anything here, AND NO PLANS
  FOR ONE" — the second clause becomes false the moment an audit is
  planned as a marketplace credential; delete the clause when true.
- NOT_CLAIMED: "one operator, one key... wrong root of trust for
  anything load-bearing" — true, and the marketplace makes us
  load-bearing. The words don't change; the FACTS must (successor
  key is already on /becoming's WATCHED list with its trigger; the
  pivot is the trigger firing).
- `what.ts` "Is this a scam?" answer: "prices are public and small"
  — "small" again; also "the top is a person's labor" stops being
  true when a $25 audit tops the shelf.
- HOUSE_RULES 21 (barbell) — Collision 3.
- `wind-down.ts` — written for a shop whose obligations end at
  delivery. Marketplace obligations (active watches, badge
  reliance) need a wind-down sentence each.

=====================================================================
PART 5 — RULE 41 REVIEW. What the pivot exposes the keeper to.
=====================================================================

Rule 41 requires this section to exist and to be loud. New exposure
classes, in descending severity:

1. **A wrong "safe" badge.** A skill we badge that exfiltrates
   credentials creates a damages claim with our signature on the
   evidence. This is the largest new liability in the entire memo
   and it is why "verified" must be defined as dated observation
   against published criteria (Gap 1) rather than a warranty —
   and why the badge copy needs the same lawyer-readable precision
   as the Certificate of Patronage's "entitles the holder to
   nothing whatsoever," WITHOUT gutting the product. That needle
   is threadable (attestation already threads it) but it is
   keeper-and-counsel work, not copy work.
2. **Reseller money flow** (if Decision 2(a)): funds passing through
   the store's wallet at volume changes the tax picture and may
   change the regulatory one (money transmission is a word a lawyer
   should say yes or no to, not us). Verified referral (2(b))
   avoids the class entirely.
3. **Upstream failure with our sticker on it**: a refund liability
   that scales with volume, uncapped unless the listing caps it.
4. **The infrastructure pager** (/becoming's own objection): the
   family cost of strangers' uptime depending on one person. If
   Collision 2 is reversed, the reversal must fund its own answer
   (succession, degradation, caps) — not just accept the risk.

PART 6 — THE ORDER OF OPERATIONS, RECOMMENDED.
=====================================================================

1. The six ⚑ decisions above, by the keeper, dated, in HOUSE_RULES
   and /becoming. Everything else is blocked on them — building
   marketplace features against unamended canon produces exactly
   the claim-drift the store exists to refuse.
2. Gap 1 (the definition of "verified") written BEFORE the first
   verified anything ships. It is the marketplace's /attestation.
3. The lowest-collision products first, because they need no
   amendments at all: productized settlement attestation (already
   the primitive), the point-in-time x402 service audit (rule 23a
   compliant as-is), key-history anchoring as dated observation.
   These are Tier 3 of the memo and they are buildable TODAY under
   standing rules — the memo had them last; the canon says do them
   first.
4. The standing_watch-shaped conformance watch (after Decision 1).
5. Verified referral for Browser Use (after Decisions 2 and 3).
6. Verified Skills (after Gap 1 and the structural rule 41 pass —
   the keeper ruled no counsel, so the badge copy carries the whole
   shield and is engineered accordingly). Highest margin, highest
   liability, last.

The memo's instinct that the store stocked the wrong shelves is
half right. The candy was never the problem — rule 23a already knew
the real product: independent signed observation. The marketplace
is that product finally priced like what it is. What this audit
adds is the half the memo skipped: the canon that made the
observation trustworthy is the inventory, and five sentences of it
need the keeper's pen before the first new shelf goes up.
